# Flintlock v1 reference dossier

Prepared before modeling, 2026-10-26 local. Selection: **British Pattern 1742 Land Service Musket**, exterior interpretation of Colonial Williamsburg specimen **1982-9 in its surviving modified configuration**. Brown Bess is a family nickname, not the variant identifier. This is a proposed representative; catalog ID and roster mapping remain unresolved. Bounded searches of the supplied handoff/docs/evidence did not recover the imported 60-entry roster; the earlier next-firearm audit reports the same omission.

## Primary evidence

The [Colonial Williamsburg object record](https://emuseum.colonialwilliamsburg.org/objects/29963/pattern-1742-land-service-musket), accessed 2026-10-26, identifies the object as Pattern 1742, England, Board of Ordnance, approximately 1742–1748. It lists iron, steel, brass and walnut. Its description supports a round barrel, curved lock plate and rounded brass fittings. The surviving specimen has a later steel ramrod and replacement upper pipe, and lacks sling swivels. These alterations must not be described as universal as-issued features. The museum gives overall length 61 1/16 inches, used only as an approximate game scale check. The broader service history predates and extends beyond this individual object's date; the game candidate is not assigned an invented individual service record.

The museum browser showed its 500px right profile (media 202873), opposite profile thumbnail (202874), and both side-detail thumbnails (202877/202878). Those images support the long slender fore-end, sloping wrist, dropped butt, exposed side lock, brass guard and opposite-side plate. The larger interactive viewer failed to display usable zoom imagery. No detailed muzzle/front photograph was successfully viewed. The [Canadian War Museum exhibition](https://www.warmuseum.ca/cwm/exhibitions/gallery1/clash5_e.html) independently identifies Long Land Pattern 1742, object CWM 19920116-104; it is contextual corroboration, not the selected specimen or a source for swapping details.

All geometry and texture pixels will be original authored work.

The reference does not establish exact hidden cross-sections, muzzle rim bevel, blind-cavity depth, wear distribution, attachment dimensions, motion limits or animation timing. These remain artistic game presentation choices. Fine inscriptions and specimen damage are omitted instead of fabricated. Other Brown Bess patterns and modern reproductions are not substitutes for this configuration.

## Bounded art plan and contract

One LOD0 editable exterior candidate: rounded longitudinal walnut stock, round steel outer form, a genuine open front rim with short blind visual cavity, shaped side lock, opposite brass plate, open guard, butt furniture and visible stored ramrod. Separate cock, frizzen and trigger groups permit limited visual articulation review. No internal mechanism, working bore, manufacturing data, loading procedure or functional firearm simulation is supplied.

Meters; +X toward muzzle, +Y up, +Z right; root at the authored breech reference frame. Named right/left hand, primary/support grip, muzzle VFX, front sight and rear alignment-reference anchors use the existing project convention. The rear alignment reference is an abstract game sight-line target, not a claim that a physical rear sight exists. A bayonet-region attachment datum may be reserved, but the attachment allowlist stays empty: no bayonet model/fit or sling configuration is qualified in this batch, and modern mounts are excluded.

Actual saved-GLB muzzle topology, off-axis GPU images, surface joins and sampled moving-group clearances must be reviewed. Hand fit, a historical action cycle, target-event integration, destination materials, collision, both destination renders and owner acceptance remain separate pending gates. No LOD is justified until the first candidate has close-view approval and measured distance requirements. Both machines are under load; no timing or FPS verdict is allowed.
