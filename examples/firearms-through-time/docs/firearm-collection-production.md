# Firearm collection production

User direction, 26 October 2026: develop the collection across all eras using proper references and documentation, with high-quality assets and historically appropriate modularity and attachments. The imported 60-entry catalog remains a proposed roster; it is not 60 completed or independently verified models.

## Required reference dossier

Every entry must identify the precise model, variant, production/service period and configuration. Keep collector nicknames separate from documented identities. Record primary museum accession/catalog records, manufacturer archives, institutional technical/historical documentation and usable photographs from multiple angles. Track URLs, access dates, attribution, image rights, and which visible feature or claim each reference supports. Generated concept images guide environment art direction only; they are not historical firearm evidence. Do not redistribute restricted reference photography inside asset packages.

Reconcile conflicting sources and mark unobserved surfaces or mechanisms as uncertain. A family resemblance or a generic name is insufficient for an exact-replica claim. Distinguish reference-backed facts, reconstruction estimates, gameplay tuning and unknowns in the asset and behavior manifests. Documentation must not imply that the game is a validated real-world ballistic simulator.

## Era coverage and sequencing

Maintain a coverage matrix spanning early ignition systems, flintlock-era arms, percussion-era arms, early cartridge and repeating arms, early service automatics, the major twentieth-century military periods, and contemporary platforms. These are research/production lanes, not a final list of models or historically rigid date boundaries. Audit the imported roster against the matrix before claiming complete coverage.

Complete representative mechanisms through modeling, animation, interaction, target feedback and both destinations before multiplying their variants. The current generic 1911-style pistol is an integration prototype. The Winchester 1866-inspired sporting-form candidate has saved variants, but is not yet an integrated military catalog entry or an animated production weapon. Broad coverage remains the destination; these prototypes validate the delivery process.

## Modularity and attachment contract

Share parts within documented model/platform families only when compatible. Era alone never grants compatibility. Keep stable IDs and named coordinate frames for visible assemblies, moving components, hand contacts, sight alignment, muzzle effects and accessory mounts. Preserve them through art revisions and LODs where they remain functional. Attachments require their own reference dossier, explicit accepted configurations/exclusions, fit review, hand-clearance checks, animation consequences and documented game-profile changes. Separate asset appearance from assembly configuration, simulation state and presentation.

## Quality and delivery gate

Each entry progresses through reference-ready, art candidate, rig/animation candidate, integrated, reviewed and production-qualified states. Required evidence includes coherent silhouette/proportions, visible muzzle cavity, deliberate material construction and wear, clean normals/UVs/mips, moving-part separation, hand contact, inspect/reload/fire presentation, attachment fit, target-local hit event integration, and distance-detail/texture budgets. Inspect close-up, aiming and third-person views in Unreal and Holodeck. A saved Kiln revision or structural QA pass does not prove final visual quality. See [hero geometry requirements](hero-firearm-geometry-requirements.md) and [shot feedback fidelity](shot-feedback-fidelity.md).

Record exact source/GLB/material revisions, reference provenance, triangle and draw costs, texture residency estimates, and destination-specific results. FPS and timing remain dirty while PC and hub are under concurrent load. Shared Kiln extension tools should enforce these contracts without making Unreal-specific features mandatory for other users.
